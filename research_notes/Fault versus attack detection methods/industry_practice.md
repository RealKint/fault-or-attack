# Industry Practice: Detecting and Triaging Cyberattacks vs Equipment Faults in OT/ICS (Oil and Gas Focus)

Research date: 2026-11-08. Sources are a mix of primary (MITRE, CISA, Microsoft Learn docs, Nozomi technical docs, INL/DOE) and vendor marketing (Dragos blogs, Claroty/Asimily blogs). Vendor-marketing claims are labelled as such.

## Q1. How do commercial OT monitoring products detect threats, and do any classify fault vs attack?

### Takeaway
Commercial products (Dragos, Nozomi, Microsoft Defender for IoT) use passive network capture plus protocol deep packet inspection, then layer several detection engines: learned baselines (assets, comms, process variable values), protocol-spec violations, known-malware/IOC signatures, and adversary-behaviour (TTP) analytics. None found explicitly outputs a binary "FAULT vs ATTACK" label; instead they tag each alert with an engine/category (e.g. Microsoft's "Operational" engine vs "Malware"/"Policy violation") and an ATT&CK for ICS technique, and their alert text often says outright "might be an operational issue or an attempt to manipulate the device", leaving the final call to a human. That gap is exactly the niche "Fault or Attack" fills.

### Cited Findings

**Dragos Platform**
- Dragos divides detection into four types: Configuration, Modeling, Indicator, and Threat Behavior, and states there is no single "best" type; Modeling is the anomaly-style approach (define normal, measure deviation) — [Dragos: Four Types of Threat Detection whitepaper (2018)](https://www.manufacturing.net/home/whitepaper/13245081/dragos-the-four-types-of-threat-detection)
- Dragos says the Platform uses threat behavior analytics (sequences of events built on adversary tradecraft, not single atomic values) as its *primary* detection method, and asset/communications baselining as a complement — [Dragos Threat Detection page](https://dragos.com/cybersecurity-platform/threat-detection) (vendor marketing)
- Dragos argues anomaly detection alone produces "a high volume of false positives" that can overwhelm analysts; Configuration detection builds a baseline of communications/devices and alerts on deviations, but is mostly used for hunting/forensics — [Dragos blog on NERC CIP-015](https://www.dragos.com/blog/nerc-cip-015-compliance-detect-anomalous-activity-with-dragos-platform) (vendor claim, not independent test)
- The same Dragos blog notes modeling detections can reveal "misconfigurations or failing assets" as well as malicious actions, i.e. one anomaly engine surfaces both faults and attacks — [Dragos blog](https://www.dragos.com/blog/nerc-cip-015-compliance-detect-anomalous-activity-with-dragos-platform)

**Nozomi Networks Guardian (documented capability)**
- N2OS has two independent learning goals, *network* and *process*; process learning covers variables and their behaviour. Each can be in Learning or Protection mode; after the learning period it auto-switches to protection — [Nozomi N2OS docs: learning settings](https://technicaldocs.nozominetworks.com/products/n2os/topics/administration/settings/scp/c_n2os_admin_settings_scp_edit_learning.html)
- Alert VI:PROC:NEW-VALUE ("New OT variable value", HIGH): a variable set to a value never seen before; recommended action is to validate and either learn it (legitimate) or treat as anomaly — [Nozomi N2OS alert reference: Virtual Image](https://technicaldocs.nozominetworks.com/products/n2os/topics/autogenerated/security_features/r_n2os_alerts_virtual-image.html)
- Nozomi FAQ claims "Dynamic Learning" uses statistical process control and discards behaviour beyond one standard deviation — [Nozomi FAQ](https://nozominetworks.com/cybersecurity-faqs/how-is-ai-used-in-ot-ics-cybersecurity) (marketing-level; exact algorithm undocumented)

**Microsoft Defender for IoT (documented capability; best public example of an "operational vs security" split)**
- Five detection engines: Policy violation (deviation from learned/configured baseline), Protocol violation (packet structures/field values violating ICS protocol spec), **Operational** ("operational issues such as intermittent connectivity that can indicate early signs of equipment failure"), Malware (known industrial malware incl. Triton, Havex, BlackEnergy), Anomaly (unusual M2M comms, e.g. a device scanning when not defined as a scanner) — [Microsoft Learn: Defender for IoT alert reference](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/defender-for-iot/alert-engine-messages)
- Each alert carries severity, category, ATT&CK for ICS tactic/technique, and whether it is "learnable" — [same](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/defender-for-iot/alert-engine-messages). Examples with explicit thresholds:
  - "Device is Suspected to be Disconnected (Unresponsive)" — 8 failed attempts in 5 min, Medium, mapped to T0881 Service Stop
  - "Suspicion of Unresponsive MODBUS Device" — Low, T0881
  - "Abnormal Exception Pattern in Slave" — 20 exceptions/hour, Low; text says "might be the result of an operational issue"; mapped to T0806 Brute Force I/O
  - "Excessive Restart Rate of an Outstation" — 10 restarts/hour; "might be the result of an operational issue or an attempt to manipulate the device"; T0814 Denial of Service
  - "Abnormal Termination of Applications" — 20 stop commands in 3 h; same dual wording; T0889 Modify Program, T0831 Manipulation of Control
  - "Excessive Malformed Packets In a Single Session" — "might indicate erroneous communications, or an attempt to manipulate"
  - "Unknown Object Sent to Outstation" / "Illegal BACNet message" — mapped to T0855 Unauthorized Command Message / T0836 Modify Parameter
  - "Controller Stop" — mapped to T0858 Change Operating Mode, T0814
  - "Firmware Version Changed" — "might be authorized activity, for example a planned maintenance procedure"
  - "Field Device Discovered Unexpectedly", "Abnormal Periodic Behavior In Communication Channel" (change in polling frequency), "Abnormal Traffic Bandwidth"
- Category list includes "Operational Issues", "Unresponsive", "Restart/Stop Commands", "Illegal Commands", "Firmware change", "Suspicion of malicious activity" — [same](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/defender-for-iot/alert-engine-messages)
- All engines except Malware alert only on OT subnets/protocols; reference intended for mapping alerts to playbooks — [Microsoft Learn summary via search](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/defender-for-iot/organizations/alerts)

**Government "cyber or not" triage method (closest to an explicit fault-vs-attack classifier)**
- DOE CESER + Idaho National Laboratory's CyOTE program "seeks to tie anomalies in operations to a cyberattack"; vocabulary: *observable* -> *anomaly* (differs from expected) -> *triggering event* (anomaly meriting investigation) -> comprehension -> decision — [DOE CyOTE fact sheet](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2021-09/CyOTE_Fact_Sheet_508.pdf); [DOE CESER CyOTE page](https://www.energy.gov/ceser/cybersecurity-operational-technology-environment)
- INL's OPTIC (Operational Process for Trigger Identification and Comprehension, July 2024) helps OT staff determine whether an anomaly "may indicate possible malicious activity or merely a maintenance related irregularity", referencing ATT&CK for ICS — [INL CyOTE product page](https://inlsoftware.inl.gov/product/cyote)
- CyOTE publishes case studies mapping incidents (Maroochy, Oldsmar, Conficker) to ATT&CK for ICS — [CyOTE Case Study: Maroochy](https://cyote.inl.gov/content/uploads/24/2025/12/CyOTE-Case-Study_Maroochy.pdf); [Oldsmar](https://cyote.inl.gov/content/uploads/24/2025/12/CyOTE-Case-Study_Oldsmar.pdf)

### Inferences
- Industry consensus: a single anomaly is ambiguous; confidence comes from *corroborating* evidence (network + process + asset change) and from mapping to adversary behaviour. Our detector should combine evidence, not threshold one signal.
- Microsoft's dual-wording alerts ("operational issue OR manipulation") show vendors deliberately avoid auto-labelling. A prototype that outputs FAULT/ATTACK *with a reason, confidence and ATT&CK ID* would be a credible step beyond — but should keep an "UNCERTAIN -> escalate" state, mirroring CyOTE's "triggering event" step.
- Concrete ideas to adopt in the Python simulator:
  1. Learning phase then protection phase (Nozomi) — baseline normal ranges/rates for each variable and for network comms during first N ticks.
  2. Engine taxonomy per alert: `operational`, `protocol_violation`, `policy_violation`, `anomaly`, `malware/IOC` (Microsoft).
  3. Rate thresholds like "X events in Y minutes" (Microsoft uses e.g. 20 exceptions/hr, 10 restarts/hr).
  4. "New value never seen" / "new talker never seen" alerts (Nozomi VI:PROC:NEW-VALUE; Microsoft "Field Device Discovered Unexpectedly").
  5. Physics cross-check: compare reported level vs. level implied by pump on/off, motor current, outflow (mass balance). Physical faults usually produce physically *consistent* but abnormal signals across sensors; attacks usually create *inconsistency* between redundant/independent channels or coincide with network anomalies (new source, unauthorized write, timing change).

### Gaps
- Claroty (xDome/CTD), Siemens SINEC Security Monitor, and Industrial Defender detection internals were not retrieved in this pass; no cited claims for them here. Claroty's blog on the SANS 5 controls was located but not fetched.
- No vendor publicly documents an automatic binary fault-vs-attack classifier; absence noted, not proven.
- Dragos's claim of fewer false positives with behaviour analytics is not independently verified.

## Q2. MITRE ATT&CK for ICS: which techniques map to our attacks and what detections are recommended?

### Takeaway
ATT&CK for ICS is MITRE's knowledge base of adversary tactics/techniques against ICS (tactics include Inhibit Response Function and Impair Process Control; Impact). Our attacks map cleanly to existing technique IDs, but MITRE's own detection guidance for the "view" techniques is thin — for Manipulation of View, MITRE states no standard detection method currently exists — so detection relies on comparing protocol content against expected values / out-of-band process data, which is precisely a physics cross-check.

### Cited Findings
- Technique mapping (IDs as used by MITRE and vendor references):
  - **T0856 Spoof Reporting Message** (fake sensor value reported) — detection data source: Network Traffic Content; analytic AN1879 says spoofed reporting messages may be detected by reviewing automation-protocol content against expected values or out-of-band process data, and to watch for Rogue Master / Adversary-in-the-Middle and LLMNR/NBT-NS poisoning that may precede it — [MITRE T0856](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T0856); [MITRE DET0746](https://attack.mitre.org/detectionstrategies/DET0746)
  - Possible renumbering: a third-party mirror says T0856 is revoked and superseded by T1692.002 "Reporting Message" in the current release — [Glexia mirror](https://security.glexia.com/learn/attack/techniques/T1692.002-reporting-message). **Unverified on attack.mitre.org; check before citing in pitch.**
  - **T0832 Manipulation of View** (operator HMI shows false data) — MITRE detection strategy DET0785 states no standard detection method currently exists (page last modified 12 May 2026) — [MITRE DET0785](https://attack.mitre.org/detectionstrategies/DET0785); definition per mirror: manipulating information reported back to operators or controllers — [Glexia T0832](https://security.glexia.com/learn/attack/techniques/T0832-manipulation-of-view)
  - **T0831 Manipulation of Control** — likewise no detailed detection guidance — [Glexia DET0747](https://security.glexia.com/learn/attack/detections/DET0747-detection-of-manipulation-of-control); [MITRE DET0747](https://attack.mitre.org/detectionstrategies/DET0747)
  - **T0829 Loss of View, T0813 Denial of Control, T0832** confirmed as matrix entries — [ICS matrix mirror](https://security.glexia.com/threat-intelligence/attack/matrices/ics)
  - **T0855 Unauthorized Command Message** and **T0836 Modify Parameter** — used by Microsoft to tag "Unknown Object Sent to Outstation", "Illegal BACNet message", "Illegal Protocol Version" — [Microsoft Learn alert reference](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/defender-for-iot/alert-engine-messages); T0836 used in CyOTE's impairment-of-process-control case analysis — [CyOTE Oldsmar case study](https://cyote.inl.gov/content/uploads/24/2025/12/CyOTE-Case-Study_Oldsmar.pdf)
  - **T0814 Denial of Service**, **T0881 Service Stop**, **T0806 Brute Force I/O**, **T0858 Change Operating Mode**, **T0857 System Firmware**, **T0889 Modify Program** — as tagged on Microsoft alerts — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/defender-for-iot/alert-engine-messages)
- Replay: ATT&CK has no separate "replay" technique found in this pass; the canonical example is Stuxnet's record-and-loop of sensor values (see Q4), which is an instance of spoofed reporting / manipulation of view.
- Dragos has published a whitepaper mapping industrial threats to ATT&CK for ICS — [Dragos: Mapping Industrial Cybersecurity Threats to MITRE ATT&CK for ICS](https://hub.dragos.com/hubfs/Whitepaper-Downloads/Mapping-Industrial-Cybersecurity-Threats-to-MITRE-ATTACK-for-ICS.pdf) (not fetched)

### Inferences
- Suggested mapping for the simulator's attack catalogue:
  | Simulator attack | ATT&CK for ICS | Detection idea |
  |---|---|---|
  | False level reading sent to controller | T0856 Spoof Reporting Message | level vs. mass balance from pump/outflow/motor current; new/unknown sender |
  | HMI shows normal while tank overflows | T0832 Manipulation of View | HMI value vs. independent sensor/historian |
  | Replay of old sensor data | T0856 / T0832 (Stuxnet pattern) | variance collapse/flatline, repeated sequence hash, stale timestamps/sequence numbers |
  | Rogue write to pump/valve | T0855 Unauthorized Command Message | write from unknown source or outside schedule |
  | Setpoint / alarm limit change | T0836 Modify Parameter | config change without change ticket |
  | Packet flood / controller unresponsive | T0814 DoS, T0813 Denial of Control | traffic-rate spike *coinciding* with unresponsiveness (vs. silent link loss = fault) |
- Tagging every ATTACK verdict with an ATT&CK ID is cheap and instantly credible to OT security judges.

## Q3. What do IEC 62443, NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3 and the SANS ICS Five Critical Controls say about monitoring and incident response?

### Takeaway
All three treat continuous, OT-protocol-aware monitoring and an OT-specific incident response plan as core, with *maintaining safe operation* prioritized over everything else. Response guidance stresses pre-planned contingency procedures to operate safely or shut down when the ICS "may be working against the process" — i.e., fall back to manual/known-safe states.

### Cited Findings
- **SANS ICS Five Critical Controls** (Robert M. Lee and Tim Conway, Oct 2022): (1) ICS-specific incident response, (2) defensible architecture, (3) ICS network visibility & monitoring, (4) secure remote access, (5) risk-based vulnerability management — [Claroty blog summary](https://blog.claroty.com/blog/the-sans-five-ics-cybersecurity-critical-controls-what-they-are-how-to-apply-them); [Dragos five critical controls](https://www.dragos.com/insights/five-critical-controls)
- Control 3 calls for continuous network security monitoring of the ICS environment with protocol-aware toolsets and "systems of systems interaction analysis" — [SANS whitepaper copy](https://ciso2ciso.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/SANS_5_Critical_Controls.pdf) (fetch blocked; wording via search snippet); passive collection of ICS traffic is a core requirement — [Asimily guide](https://asimily.com/blog/implementing-the-sans-5-critical-ics-cybersecurity-controls-a-practical-guide/)
- SANS webcast on Control 3 lists benefits including safety asset identification, operational safety and reliability, and *engineering troubleshooting* — i.e., the same visibility serves fault diagnosis — [SANS webcast](https://www.sans.org/webcasts/sans-5-ics-critical-controls-for-electric-control-3---ics-network-visibility-monitoring)
- **NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3** (final 2023) is structured as an OT overlay of SP 800-53 Rev. 5 control baselines — [Dragos NIST 800-82r3 solution brief](https://dragos.brightspotcdn.com/e7/fc/3c2374f14b8591fc742bbde86e1f/nist-800-82r3-solution-brief-01-25.pdf); continuous monitoring and incident response are central tenets — [Industrial Cyber guide](https://industrialcyber.co/nist/the-essential-guide-to-the-nist-sp-800-82-document/); secondary summary: ICS response should put maintaining/restoring safe operation above other response objectives — [Opsio guide](https://opsiocloud.com/blogs/nist-800-82-ot-security-guide/) (secondary, quality mixed)
- **IEC 62443-3-3** system requirements relevant here: SR 3.6 Deterministic output (outputs go to a predefined state when normal operation can't be maintained) — [UpGuard summary](https://www.upguard.com/blog/isa-62443-3-3-2013); SR 6.2 Continuous monitoring (FR 6 Timely response to events) — [ISA 62443-3-3 TOC](https://www.isa.org/getmedia/4379f3a1-2687-4a0a-9d0f-20d743233c1b/ISA-62443-3-3_TOC.pdf); SR 7.1 Denial of service protection (FR 7 Resource availability) — [OT Academy overview](https://ot-academy-f7e18a.pages.dev/iec62443.html); component-level CR 6.2 test includes verifying essential functions stay available during DoS — [IEC 62443-4-2 assessment docs](https://cip-documents.readthedocs.io/en/latest/iec-62443-assessment/iec-62443-4-2/iec-62443-4-2-fr-6-fr-7.html)
- **CISA (Ukraine alert) response guidance**: develop and exercise contingency plans to safely operate or shut down processes when the ICS is breached, assuming "the ICS may be working against the process"; perform impact analysis before defensive measures; isolate ICS from untrusted networks; operator-controlled, time-limited remote access; MFA; application whitelisting on HMIs — [CISA IR-ALERT-H-16-056-01](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-alerts/ir-alert-h-16-056-01)

### Inferences
- Playbook for the prototype (per verdict):
  - **FAULT**: raise maintenance work order, switch affected loop to redundant sensor or manual control, keep network as is, log for reliability (MTBF).
  - **ATTACK**: (1) ensure safety first — place process in safe state / manual local control (IEC SR 3.6 deterministic output; CISA "operate safely or shut down"); (2) stop trusting the network-reported value, use independent/local gauge; (3) isolate the offending source / segment (SANS Control 2 defensible architecture), block remote access; (4) preserve evidence (pcap, historian); (5) notify (CSIRT, and in Brunei, Cyber Security Brunei per CII duty — see Q5).
  - **UNCERTAIN**: treat as CyOTE "triggering event" — escalate to joint ops + security review.
- Pitch line: "Built around SANS Control #1 and #3 and IEC 62443 SR 6.2."

## Q4. Real incidents where attacks were mistaken for faults (or vice versa) and lessons

### Takeaway
The recurring lesson: attackers either hide behind "normal-looking" data (Stuxnet replay) or their effects look like equipment failure (Triton's first trip was blamed on a mechanical fault), while IT-only incidents can still force OT shutdowns (Colonial). Detection must cross-check process physics against reported data and investigate "unexplained trips" with a cyber lens.

### Cited Findings
- **Triton/Trisis (Petro Rabigh, Saudi Arabia, 2017)**: an earlier (June 2017) shutdown at the same plant was attributed to a mechanical failure of the Triconex ESD system; a researcher said "The June investigation was insufficient", and attackers remained undetected until the August trip — a missed chance two months earlier — [Dark Reading: Triton/Trisis more widespread](https://www.darkreading.com/cyberattacks-data-breaches/triton-trisis-attack-was-more-widespread-than-publicly-known); six controllers were infected, incl. burner management — [same]; Schneider concluded a malware development error accidentally tripped the Triconex to a safe state — [Dark Reading: Schneider on 0-day](https://www.darkreading.com/vulnerabilities---threats/schneider-electric-triton-trisis-attack-used-0-day-flaw-in-its-safety-controller-system-and-a-rat/d/d-id/1330845); public disclosure by FireEye and Dragos, Dec 2017 — [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/triton-ics-malware-fireeye-dragos/)
- **Stuxnet (Natanz, ~2009-2010)**: recorded ~21 seconds of normal cascade protection sensor values and replayed them in a loop during the attack, so operators and software alarms saw normal; legitimate code kept running on fake inputs — [Foreign Policy: Stuxnet's Secret Twin (Langner)](https://foreignpolicy.com/2013/11/19/stuxnets-secret-twin/); speed manipulation to ~1410 Hz from nominal ~1064 Hz — [exida blog](https://www.exida.com/Blog/grasping-the-power-of-the-stuxnet-virus-or-what-i-did-during-the-covid-19) (pre-2017 incident, but foundational for replay detection)
- **Ukraine grid, Dec 2015**: ~225,000 customers lost power; attackers remotely operated breakers via legitimate remote admin / ICS client software over VPN with stolen credentials; KillDisk wiped systems incl. HMIs; serial-to-Ethernet firmware corrupted; UPS disconnects scheduled to hamper restoration; attacks within 30 min across sites — [CISA IR-ALERT-H-16-056-01](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-alerts/ir-alert-h-16-056-01)
- **Colonial Pipeline (May 2021)**: ransomware hit corporate IT (including billing), not OT, yet the company shut OT operations "out of an abundance of caution"; restart was slow due to lack of cold-start procedures — [CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/3618016/colonial-pipeline-shutdown-highlights-need-for-better-ot-cybersecurity-practices.html); [CyberWire two years later](https://thecyberwire.com/stories/298c278398dc42548d02876a99373345/the-colonial-pipeline-ransomware-attack-two-years-later); commentators argue the decision was driven as much by inability to bill as by cyber risk — [R Street](https://www.rstreet.org/commentary/it-vs-ot-national-security-lessons-from-colonial-pipeline/)
- **Oldsmar water (2021)** and **Maroochy (2000)** are analysed by INL CyOTE as ATT&CK-mapped case studies — [CyOTE Oldsmar](https://cyote.inl.gov/content/uploads/24/2025/12/CyOTE-Case-Study_Oldsmar.pdf); [CyOTE Maroochy](https://cyote.inl.gov/content/uploads/24/2025/12/CyOTE-Case-Study_Maroochy.pdf)

### Inferences
- Detection lessons to build into the demo:
  - *Triton lesson*: "unexplained safety trip" should never be closed as FAULT without checking for concurrent network/engineering-workstation activity. Demo scenario: SIS trip + new engineering connection -> ATTACK.
  - *Stuxnet lesson*: a sensor that is "too normal" (no noise, repeating pattern) while physical evidence (motor current, outflow) changes is a replay signature.
  - *Ukraine lesson*: commands from legitimate tools with valid credentials still look anomalous in timing/source/coordination (simultaneous multi-asset commands).
  - *Colonial lesson*: good fault/attack triage avoids unnecessary full shutdowns — a strong business pitch for Hibiscus ("reduce false trips and downtime, not only stop hackers").
  - Inverse case (fault mistaken for attack) also costs money; framing the tool as reducing both error types fits the "Safety and Operational Reliability" challenge.

### Gaps
- Exact date conflict: some sources date the Triton discovery trip to August 2017, others say October/December 2017 disclosure; treat "2017" as safe.
- SANS/E-ISAC Ukraine 2015 report (operator observations of cursor moving on HMI) not retrieved; avoid that detail unless verified.

## Q5. Brunei / ASEAN context

### Takeaway
Brunei has a Cybersecurity Order, 2023 that lets the Minister designate Critical Information Infrastructure (CII) and imposes duties on CII owners (codes of practice, incident reporting, audits and risk assessments) for "essential services"; Cyber Security Brunei (CSB) is the national agency and is reported to be preparing additional CII-owner guidelines. Whether energy/oil & gas is explicitly in the Schedule was not confirmed in this pass, though it is highly likely given Brunei's economy.

### Cited Findings
- Cybersecurity Order, 2023 (made under Article 83(3) of the Constitution) contains provisions for designation of CII, duty to report cybersecurity incidents for CII, cybersecurity audits and risk assessments, codes of practice/standards of performance, and a Schedule of Essential Services; the Minister named is the Minister of Transport and Infocommunications — [Cybersecurity Order 2023 text (aipalync)](https://aipalync.org/document/view/1813)
- CSB provides cybersecurity services to public and private sectors with CII protection as a priority; a Sept 2025 Brunei cyber conference reported CSB preparing new guidelines for CII owners — [US-ASEAN Business Council](https://www.usasean.org/article/brunei-stepping-cybersecurity-and-fraud-mechanisms); senior official statement on cybersecurity importance — [The Star, Jan 2025](https://thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2025/01/10/cybersecurity-crucial-for-brunei-as-it-ensures-secure-and-trusted-environment-for-nation-says-top-official); [Borneo Bulletin, Jan 2025](https://borneobulletin.com.bn/securing-bruneis-cyberspace)
- IEA policy database lists energy-sector CII duties (furnishing information, codes of practice, incident reporting, audits) under a "Cybersecurity Act" entry — [IEA policy 26609](https://www.iea.org/policies/26609) — but it is unclear whether this entry is Brunei's or another ASEAN state's; treat with care.
- Regional comparators: Singapore Cybersecurity Act 2018 amendments in force 31 Oct 2025 — [Rajah & Tann](https://www.rajahtannasia.com/viewpoints/cybersecurity-act-amendments-on-provider-owned-critical-information-infrastructure-and-systems-of-temporary-cybersecurity-concern-in-force-from-31-october-2025/); Malaysia Cyber Security Act 2024 (NCII sectors) — [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/report/malaysia-the-cyber-security-bill-2024/)

### Inferences
- Pitch angle: under the Cybersecurity Order 2023 a CII owner must report cybersecurity incidents — so fast, evidence-backed fault-vs-attack triage directly supports the reporting duty (you must know it's an attack to report it, and avoid over-reporting faults). Have the prototype generate an incident-report stub (time, asset, ATT&CK technique, evidence) for ATTACK verdicts.
