# Academic and published methods for telling physical faults from cyberattacks in ICS/CPS

Scope note: this covers methods that **classify the cause** (fault or attack), not only detect anomalies. Each technique entry lists the core idea, the signal, why it separates faults from attacks, limitations, the source, and a note on adding it to the "Fault or Attack" single-tank Python simulator (pump, on/off level control at 40/60%, sensor A, optional backup B, motor feedback, outflow meter, power monitor, packet counts). Full texts were read for Urbina et al. (CCS 2016), Xue et al. (arXiv 2025), Ahsan et al. (arXiv 2026 / IEEE TSG) and Zhang, Kasis, Polycarpou & Parisini (IFAC SAFEPROCESS 2022). Other entries come from abstracts, repository pages and search snippets. Those are marked "(abstract-level)".

## Q1. What are the main published approaches to telling faults from attacks?

### Takeaway
The literature has about eight families. The most implementable for a single tank are: (1) model-based residuals with a **stateful (CUSUM) test** on a mass-balance model; (2) **two-sided / dual residual checks** (controller-side vs plant-side consistency, where faults trip one and stealthy attacks trip the other); (3) **active probing / watermarking** to expose replay; (4) **cyber + physical evidence fusion** (network/log anomalies must co-occur for an "attack" label); and (5) a small **Bayesian network** for combining evidence. Fault/attack trees and multi-mode switched models are mostly design-time or diagnostic frameworks. Heavy ML (deep nets, ensemble classifiers) mainly appears in power-grid work and gives modest accuracy on realistic data.

### Cited Findings

**A. Model-based residuals + stateful statistical tests (CUSUM). Urbina et al., ACM CCS 2016**
- Core idea: predict the physical output with a model, form the residual r_k = y_k − ŷ_k, and test it. A *stateless* test alarms when |r_k| > τ. A *stateful* test accumulates S_k of past residuals "no matter how small" and alarms on persistent deviation. Options named are window average, EWMA and nonparametric CUSUM — [Urbina et al. 2016, CCS](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/%7Ealacarde/papers/ccs16.pdf)
- Key result: "while the vast majority of previous work uses stateless tests on residuals, stateful tests are better in limiting the impact of stealthy attackers (for the same levels of false alarms)". Impact also depends on the control algorithm, not only on the detector — [Urbina et al. 2016](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/%7Ealacarde/papers/ccs16.pdf)
- **Directly relevant water-tank case**: the authors model the SWaT tank level from first principles as an LDS, h_{k+1} = h_k + (Q_in − Q_out)/Area, with on/off pump and valve. The attacker raises the *reported* level more slowly than the real level so the PLC's "High" cutoff fires late, aiming for overflow. They set the CUSUM bias b = 0.002 so S_k stays near zero without attack and measured false alarms over an 8 h attack-free run. Across 20 undetected 40-minute attacks, the stateless test still let the tank spill unless the thresholds were tiny (many false alarms). The CUSUM test avoided spilling with a threshold that gave E[T_fa] ≈ 170 min — [Urbina et al. 2016](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/%7Ealacarde/papers/ccs16.pdf)
- They also attacked the pump so it ignores OFF commands while reporting OFF (the same pattern as our "replay with faked feedback" / "pump running without command"). Only the stateful test detected it — [Urbina et al. 2016](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/%7Ealacarde/papers/ccs16.pdf)
- Why it helps fault vs attack: a stealthy attacker is forced to "follow more closely our Physical Model", which caps the damage per unit time. Urbina does **not** itself classify fault vs attack. It bounds what an attacker can do while staying undetected — [Urbina et al. 2016](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/%7Ealacarde/papers/ccs16.pdf)
- Limitation admitted: if the attacker controls all sensors *and* actuators, residuals can stay at zero while the plant is driven anywhere — [Urbina et al. 2016](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/%7Ealacarde/papers/ccs16.pdf)
- Survey context: Giraldo et al., "A Survey of Physics-Based Attack Detection in Cyber-Physical Systems", ACM Computing Surveys 51(4), 2018, doi 10.1145/3203245. Its taxonomy covers attack detection, attack location and validation — [NIST CSRC record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/journal/2018/07/a-survey-of-physicsbased-attack-detection-in-cps/final). The precursor is NIST GCR 16-010 (2016) — [NIST](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/gcr/2016/NIST.GCR.16-010.pdf)
- **Sim note:** we already have a "steady mass-balance error (linear running total)". Replace or augment it with a proper nonparametric CUSUM: `S = max(0, S + abs(r) - b); alarm if S > tau; reset S=0 on alarm`, where r = measured Δlevel − (pump_in − outflow)/Area. Tune b just above the normal noise |r| and set tau from a fault-free run (target, e.g., one false alarm per N simulated hours). Also log the "max level deviation achievable by an undetected attack" (Urbina's metric) as a robustness score.

**B. Dual (controller-side + plant-side) residuals: "faults trip one detector, stealthy attacks trip the other". Xue, Shen, Ding & Zhao, arXiv 2510.14052, Oct 2025**
- Core idea: a classic observer-based fault detector sits on the controller side. A second detector on the plant side predicts what the controller *should* have commanded, û = G(y), and compares it with the input actually received, u_a. Stealthy ("kernel") attacks such as zero-dynamics, covert and replay fit the plant's kernel space, so the controller-side detector misses them. They are not built to fit the controller's dynamics, so the plant-side detector catches them — [Xue et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14052)
- Discrimination logic: "Kernel attacks only trigger responses in the plant-side detector." A fault yields y_f and a control input u = G(y_f) that is consistent with the controller, so "faults are only detected by the controller-side detector." Joint analysis of the two residuals separates them — [Xue et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14052)
- Needs no extra encryption, authentication signal or plant modification. The stealthy-attack feasible set shrinks because attacks must satisfy an extra "closed-loop stealthiness" constraint — [Xue et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14052)
- Limitations admitted: if a replay attack and a fault occur together, only the plant-side detector responds, and replayed data can mask the fault (Remark 5, which suggests adding active methods such as watermarking). The plant side usually lacks the real-time reference, so they assume a zero or fixed-pattern setpoint (Remark 6). It requires a reconstructable controller. Evaluation is simulation only (linearized UAV model, RLC circuit), with no real dataset — [Xue et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14052)
- **Sim note:** our controller is a known on/off rule (ON < 40%, OFF > 60%, hysteresis). On the "plant side" (pump/actuator), recompute the expected command from the level the pump *physically* has (backup sensor B or a model-estimated level) and compare it with the command the pump actually received. Rule: controller-side residual only (sensor/plant disagrees with the model, command consistent with reported level) → FAULT. Plant-side command residual only (received command inconsistent with what the controller law would output from the true level) → ATTACK. This generalizes our "pump running without command" and "command injection" checks.

**C. Two-filter + unknown-input-observer FDI. Taheri, Khorasani, Shames & Meskin, IEEE TCST 32(2), 2024 (arXiv 2009.06196)** (abstract-level)
- Uses filters on both the plant side and the command-and-control side, plus a UIO-based detector on the plant side, to detect *and isolate* both cyberattacks and machine-induced faults. It characterizes detectability conditions for covert, zero-dynamics and replay attacks. A modified version removes the need for any secure channel — [author page](https://mtaheriee.github.io/publication/cyberattack-machine-induced-fdi-tcst); [arXiv 2009.06196](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2009.06196)
- The authors stress "an inherent difference between machine induced faults and cyber attacks", so plain fault-detection methods are insufficient — [arXiv 2009.06196](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2009.06196)
- **Sim note:** same structure as B. A UIO is overkill for one state. A Luenberger-style 1-state observer, `h_hat += dt*(Qin_cmd - Qout_meas)/A + L*(y - h_hat)`, is enough.

**D. Active probing / physical watermarking for replay.** Mo & Sinopoli (Allerton 2009) onward; Zhang, Keliris, Parisini & Polycarpou (IEEE L-CSS 2022); Zhang, Kasis, Polycarpou & Parisini (IFAC SAFEPROCESS 2022)
- Mo & Sinopoli (2009) first modelled replay attacks on control systems. Their countermeasure adds a known random signal to the control input and checks that sensor data still respond to it. Replayed data does not. This degrades control performance, so there is a trade-off — [arXiv 2103.00790 (cites the lineage)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.00790); [online watermark design, arXiv 1911.01868](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.01868)
- Zhang et al. 2022 (L-CSS 6:1178–1183) separates *sensor replay attacks* from *sensor bias faults* with a specially designed watermark plus adaptive estimation. Both threats sit on the same sensor-to-controller channel, so ordinary detectors struggle (abstract-level) — [arts.units.it record](https://arts.units.it/handle/11368/2993775); [dblp](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/csysl/ZhangKPP22)
- Zhang, Kasis, Polycarpou & Parisini (IFAC SAFEPROCESS 2022) extend this to general process, actuator and sensor faults. They use a "sensor additive switching watermark" and a **two-layer decision**: layer 1 detects any threat against a nominal adaptive threshold. Layer 2 uses a second, fault-scenario threshold, and the watermark seeds are designed so the residual exceeds it *only* under replay. They note the earlier L-CSS method handled only constant bias faults. They assume simultaneous replay and bias faults are very unlikely, and evaluation is simulation only — [Zhang et al. 2022, Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/7263349)
- **Sim note:** cheap and strong. While the pump is ON, add a small pseudo-random ±δ dither to pump speed (or make tiny jitters in ON/OFF timing). Correlate the dither with Δ(sensor A) over a window. Real data (healthy or faulty sensor that still tracks the level) shows the correlation. Replayed or frozen data shows ~zero correlation. Combine it with our flatline check: flatline with no watermark response and a *healthy* B → stuck sensor (FAULT). Values still varying "naturally" but uncorrelated with the dither → REPLAY (ATTACK). This is stronger than exact-repeat matching, which a smart replayer defeats by adding noise.

**E. Fusing cyber evidence (network, logs, code paths) with physical measurements**
- Ahsan, Wang, Motakatla, Zhu & Liu (Penn State / NREL), arXiv 2601.03289 (2026, stated as IEEE Trans. Smart Grid). They build taint/dependency graphs of the DER management application code and match patterns across PMU data, network PCAPs and application logs. Logs consistent with field deviation → system fault. Network data inconsistent with field data → FDI. Unexpected deviation inside program nodes → memory-corruption attack — [arXiv 2601.03289](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03289)
- Mosaffa & Khaloozadeh (SCADA, OMNeT++ simulation). A fault-detection filter flags anomalies, and an "attack" is declared only if network parameters (latency, packet loss, traffic) are also abnormal (abstract-level) — [magiran 2466014](https://magiran.com/paper/2466014/?lang=en)
- A building-energy hardware-in-the-loop study pairs a network analyzer with a physical fault detector in a joint classifier (abstract-level) — [ScienceDirect, Energy & Buildings 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378778824007126); [OSTI preprint](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/2458230)
- On SWaT, combining network traffic with process data improved attack-classification recall in a proof-of-concept study — [NIKT paper](https://ntnu.no/ojs/index.php/nikt/article/download/6242/5569/23743)
- A US patent describes coupling anomalies in physical data with anomalies in cyber communications over time to separate faults from attacks — [USPTO 12560639](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12560639); see also [USPTO 10841322 "Decision system and method for separating faults from attacks"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10841322)
- **Sim note:** we already use packet counts and unknown-host writes. Add a **temporal-coupling rule**: a physical anomaly that begins within ±k steps of a network anomaly (new source, write burst, packet-rate change, Modbus write to the setpoint/coil register) gets ATTACK probability raised. A physical anomaly with a quiet network gets FAULT. Also log a simple "command log vs observed actuator state" consistency check, which is the log-consistency pattern from Ahsan et al.

**F. Bayesian networks combining technical and contextual evidence. Chockalingam, Pieters, Teixeira & van Gelder, *Cybersecurity* 4:29, 2021**
- A BN, built and validated with domain experts, that helps floodgate operators decide whether *incorrect sensor measurements* come from an intentional attack or an accidental technical failure, so they choose the right response — [TU Delft repository](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:5926ebb8-9f98-4fd9-98cc-e670990def83); [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/7129cecd3dd0462eb374daa0d4daba6b)
- Its variables include physical access control, sensor-data integrity verification and maintenance state, with a root node "intentional attack vs accidental technical failure". The model is distributed in the R package bnRep as `intentionalattacks` — [rdrr.io bnRep](https://rdrr.io/cran/bnRep/man/intentionalattacks.html). Probability elicitation was published separately (2023, J. Information Security and Applications), and an earlier "attack-failure distinguisher" framework appeared in the thesis — [TU Delft thesis record](https://research.tudelft.nl/en/publications/distinguishing-attacks-and-failures-in-industrial-control-systems)
- **Sim note:** a naive-Bayes or hand-built BN in pure Python (dict of conditional probability tables). Inputs are our existing boolean checks plus context nodes (maintenance due, sensor age, recent firmware change, remote-access session open). Output is P(attack | evidence) instead of a hard rule. This also gives a confidence score for the UI. Hand-set CPTs are fine for a demo, which is how the paper did it (expert elicitation).

**G. Multi-mode / switched-model diagnosis. Provan & Orizio, CENICS 2017** (abstract-level)
- The CPS is modelled as a discrete-time linear system that switches between modes, with each fault and each attack encoded as its own mode. Model-based diagnosis then isolates which mode explains the data. Demonstrated on a hydraulic benchmark — [CENICS 2017](https://personales.upv.es/thinkmind/CENICS/CENICS_2017/cenics_2017_4_40_68006.html)
- **Sim note:** very natural for us. Run 11 one-step-ahead predictors in parallel (nominal + 6 fault modes + 4 attack modes, each a small modification of the tank equation, e.g., leak: extra −q_leak; stuck: y const; drift: y = h + c·t; FDI: y = h + a while network shows writes). Pick the mode with the lowest accumulated squared residual, a multiple-model / bank-of-filters approach. Gives a principled "which of the 10 events" label.

**H. Attack-fault trees (design-time, not online). Kumar & Stoelinga, HASE 2017**
- "Attack fault trees" combine fault trees (safety) and attack trees (security). Leaves are basic component failures or basic attack steps. Metrics include most-likely attack path, most costly failure and expected attack impact, optionally within a time or budget — [CPS-VO biblio](https://archive.cps-vo.org/node/488/biblio/keyword/69010); [dblp](https://dblp1.uni-trier.de/rec/conf/hase/KumarS17.html). The parametric timed-automata extension is André, Lime, Ramparison & Stoelinga, Fundamenta Informaticae 2021 — [doi 10.3233/FI-2021-2066](https://doi.org/10.3233/FI-2021-2066)
- Budde, Kolb & Stoelinga find static attack trees and fault trees mathematically equivalent (snippet-level) — [arXiv 2106.06272](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.06272)
- **Sim note:** not an online detector. Useful as a documentation slide: one AFT with top event "tank overflow / pump dry-run", OR-gates over our 6 faults and 4 attacks. It also gives a priori probabilities to feed the BN in F.

**I. Data-driven classifiers (heavy ML, flag as optional)**
- Borges Hink, Beaver, Buckner, Morris, Adhikari & Pan, "Machine learning for power system disturbance and cyber-attack discrimination", ISRCS 2014 (ORNL). They compare many ML classifiers for separating natural disturbances from cyberattacks, using PMU, relay and Snort-log features — [ORNL record](https://www.ornl.gov/publication/machine-learning-power-system-disturbance-and-cyber-attack-discrimination); [OSTI](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1154827)
- A data-driven study on IEEE 30-bus fault and attack data found that many standard supervised classifiers performed poorly on realistic data (abstract-level) — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/279914429_A_Data-Driven_Approach_to_Distinguish_Cyber-Attacks_from_Physical_Faults_in_a_Smart_Grid)
- Adversarial class-imbalance learning separates attack and fault classes in interconnected CPS (Control Engineering Practice 2024, abstract-level) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0967066124003757)
- **Sim note:** needs no hardware. Pure-stdlib k-NN or a decision stump over our feature vector is feasible. Deep nets / ensembles are not needed and would need numpy or sklearn.

**J. Observer-based joint estimation (power/smart grid). Li et al., IET Smart Energy Systems 2026** (abstract-level)
- Frames the difference physically: faults appear as physical abnormalities, while attacks are crafted to *preserve nominal-looking behaviour while corrupting information flows*. Uses unknown-input observers to estimate faults and sensor attacks together — [Wiley](https://ietresearch.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1049/ses2.70028?af=R)

### Inferences
- Our rule set is already a hand-built version of families A, B, E and G. The biggest gaps relative to the literature are: (i) **no active test** (watermarking), so replay detection relies on exact-repeat matching, which an attacker defeats by adding noise; (ii) **no stateful test with tuned false-alarm rate**; (iii) **no probabilistic combination**, so rules fire as hard labels with no confidence.
- The common theme across Xue 2025, Taheri 2024, Ahsan 2026 and Mosaffa is the **consistency principle**. A fault keeps the *cyber layer* self-consistent (commands match controller logic, logs match packets) while the *physical layer* breaks. An attack makes the *cyber layer* inconsistent with physics or with the controller law. This is a good one-sentence pitch for the hackathon.

### Gaps
- I could not open the full text of Cárdenas et al. (ASIACCS 2011, "Attacks against process control systems: risk assessment, detection, and response"), so it is not cited in detail. It is the origin of the CUSUM-on-residual idea that Urbina 2016 extends (Urbina 2016 is co-authored by Cárdenas's group).
- Teixeira et al. "A secure control framework for resource-limited adversaries" (Automatica 51, 2015) is cited by Xue 2025 as showing stealthy attacks (replay, covert, zero-dynamics) evade residual detectors. Its attack-space taxonomy (model knowledge × disclosure × disruption resources) was not read in full here.
- The full texts of Taheri 2024, the Zhang L-CSS 2022 paper and the Chockalingam BN were not retrieved (403 / not opened). Details are abstract-level.

## Q2. What features or signatures do papers use to make the fault-vs-attack decision?

### Takeaway
The recurring discriminating signatures are: **which detector fires** (controller-side vs plant-side), **response to an injected probe** (watermark present or absent), **coincidence with cyber events** (network, log or code anomalies), **residual persistence and shape** (stateful accumulation, linear drift vs abrupt), **stealthiness itself** (an anomaly that stays just under thresholds while steering a safety variable is suspicious), and **contextual priors** (maintenance, access, age).

### Cited Findings
- **Stealthiness as the discriminator:** "this stealthy property of kernel attacks serves as an important indicator to distinguish attacks from faults". Faults are not crafted to fit the plant's kernel space, while attacks are — [Xue et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14052)
- **Intent / design:** "faults typically result from natural failures or component degradation, integrity attacks are intentionally designed to manipulate data and bypass fault detectors" — [Xue et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14052). Similarly, attacks "preserve nominal behaviour while corrupting information flows" — [Li et al. 2026 IET SES](https://ietresearch.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1049/ses2.70028?af=R)
- **Targeting control thresholds:** Urbina's tank attacker deliberately biases the level reading so the controller's High-level cutoff fires late. The attack is defined by the deviation between true and fake level at the moment the threshold is crossed — [Urbina et al. 2016](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/%7Ealacarde/papers/ccs16.pdf)
- **Watermark response:** the residual exceeds the second (fault-scenario) adaptive threshold only under replay — [Zhang, Kasis, Polycarpou & Parisini 2022](https://zenodo.org/records/7263349)
- **Cyber-physical consistency patterns:** logs consistent while the field deviates → fault. Network inconsistent with the field → FDI. Unexpected program-node deviation → memory corruption — [Ahsan et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03289). Network latency, packet loss and traffic as tie-breakers — [Mosaffa & Khaloozadeh](https://magiran.com/paper/2466014/?lang=en)
- **Contextual evidence:** physical access control, data-integrity verification and maintenance state as BN nodes — [bnRep intentionalattacks](https://rdrr.io/cran/bnRep/man/intentionalattacks.html)
- **Physical-fault signature for leaks:** in water distribution, conventional leaks and bursts are often detected via minimum night flow. This contrasts with attacks that keep values within historical bounds — [AQUA review (IWA)](https://iwa.silverchair.com/aqua/article-pdf/73/3/504/1392625/jws0730504.pdf)
- **Frequency-domain / time-frequency features** appear in power-electronics and microgrid work (Amini et al. 2017, frequency-domain localization; Beg et al. 2021, parametric time-frequency logic; Gupta et al. 2022, noninvasive attack/fault distinction), as cited in [Ahsan et al. 2026 related work](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03289) (not read individually)

### Inferences
- Candidate **new features** for our simulator, all stdlib-implementable:
  1. *Threshold-proximity / goal-directedness score*: fraction of anomaly time during which the reported level sits near 40% or 60%, or the anomaly onset aligns with a controller switching instant. Attacks that aim to hold the pump ON or OFF cluster there, while random faults do not.
  2. *Command-law consistency residual* (plant-side detector of Xue 2025): count of steps where the received pump command ≠ on_off_law(true/backup level, previous state).
  3. *Watermark correlation* over a sliding window (see D).
  4. *Onset coincidence* with network events (lag in steps between the first network anomaly and the first physical anomaly).
  5. *Residual shape*: fit a line to the CUSUM / running mass-balance error. A slope that stays constant means drift or leak (fault). A step change means FDI or bias. Residuals that are zero while the physical outcome is bad (overflow or dry-run seen by B, the outflow meter or the power monitor) mean a stealthy attack.
  6. *Multi-sensor agreement pattern*: A disagrees with B but B agrees with the mass balance → sensor A fault or FDI on A. Then use network evidence to break the tie.
  7. *Spectral flatness / noise variance* of sensor A: replayed or stuck data often has different noise statistics. Compare the variance of first differences against the healthy baseline. A synthetic signal can also have too little noise.

### Gaps
- I found no paper giving a quantitative "threshold-proximity" feature specifically. It is inferred from the Urbina attacker model and is not a published metric.
- Spectral discriminators were only seen in power-system citations, not verified in water/tank settings.

## Q3. How are stealthy attacks handled (e.g., attacks mimicking drift or staying below thresholds), and what limits do papers admit?

### Takeaway
No passive method can always separate a perfectly stealthy, fully informed attacker from a fault. Papers instead (a) **bound the impact** (CUSUM limits how far an undetected attacker can push the level), (b) **shrink the stealthy set** with extra independent checks (plant-side detector, redundant sensors), or (c) **actively probe** (watermarks). All admit irreducible cases: control of all sensors and actuators, simultaneous replay and fault, or observationally identical fault/attack pairs.

### Cited Findings
- Urbina's adversary knows the model, the test and the threshold, and keeps the statistic below threshold. It is adaptive, so lowering the threshold changes the attack. ROC curves therefore give 0% detection and are meaningless against it — [Urbina et al. 2016](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/%7Ealacarde/papers/ccs16.pdf)
- Proposed metric: **max deviation per unit time imposed by undetected attacks (y-axis) vs expected time between false alarms E[T_fa] (x-axis)**. E[T_fa] is used instead of false-alarm rate to avoid the base-rate fallacy — [Urbina et al. 2016](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/%7Ealacarde/papers/ccs16.pdf)
- With CUSUM, "once the CUSUM statistic reaches the threshold ... the attack no longer has enough room to continue deviating". With stateless tests, the attacker can shift the measurement by τ every step — [Urbina et al. 2016](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/%7Ealacarde/papers/ccs16.pdf)
- Better models limit impact more: on SWaT pH, nonlinear models of order 50/100 stopped the stealthy attacker from pushing pH below 5, while an order-20 LDS did not — [Urbina et al. 2016](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/%7Ealacarde/papers/ccs16.pdf)
- Fundamental limit: if the attacker controls all sensors and actuators, residuals can stay zero while the plant goes unsafe — [Urbina et al. 2016](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/%7Ealacarde/papers/ccs16.pdf)
- Pasqualetti, Dörfler & Bullo (IEEE TAC 2013) model attacks as unknown inputs to a descriptor system and characterize fundamental detection/identification limits. Undetectable attacks are tied to system zero dynamics, static detectors miss some attacks dynamic ones catch, and identification is computationally hard — [Part II, arXiv 1202.6049](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1202.6049); [Part I PDF](https://typeset.io/pdf/attack-detection-and-identification-in-cyber-physical-4l30kklqtu.pdf) (snippet-level)
- Dual detection forces attacks to satisfy an extra closed-loop stealthiness constraint, which reduces their feasible set, but simultaneous replay and fault remain ambiguous — [Xue et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14052)
- Measurement-only data cannot separate causes that produce the same observations. A 2026 *Sensors* paper on energy CPS reports chance-level discrimination for exactly matched sensor-fault/attack pairs and proposes identifiability-aware, cost-aware triage (search-snippet level, full text blocked by 403) — [Sensors 26(17):5585, 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/s26175585)
- Ahsan et al.'s case 5 shows an attacker can alter packets to trigger the "FDI" pattern instead of the "fault" pattern, i.e., the classifier itself can be gamed — [Ahsan et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03289)

### Inferences
- **For our "attack that looks like sensor drift"**: our current drift rule (steady linear divergence A vs B) would label it FAULT. Published remedies, in order of ease: (1) watermark correlation (drift-faulty sensor still responds to the dither, and a fabricated value stream generally will not unless the attacker models it); (2) goal-directedness, since real drift is indifferent to the 40/60 thresholds while an attack drift is chosen to stop the pump just before overflow (Urbina's attacker); (3) network coincidence (an FDI needs a write path); (4) BN prior on sensor age and maintenance. Add a "stealthy drift FDI" scenario and an "attacker who also spoofs B" scenario to the simulator. Report that the second is fundamentally indistinguishable without an independent channel (outflow meter, power draw), which matches the published limits.
- Adopt Urbina's metric as an honest second score next to accuracy: "worst undetected overflow margin (cm) vs false alarms per simulated day".

### Gaps
- I found no published closed-form test that specifically tells a slow-ramp FDI from a genuine linear sensor drift using passive data alone. The literature implies it is not possible without an independent channel or active probing.

## Q4. How do papers evaluate fault-vs-attack discrimination (datasets, metrics, accuracy)?

### Takeaway
Most control-theory papers (Xue 2025, Zhang 2022, Taheri 2024, Provan 2017) evaluate on **simulations only** with residual plots and no accuracy numbers. Security papers use testbeds (SWaT) and real traces. ML papers on the ORNL/MSU power dataset report accuracy around 90%. Common datasets (SWaT, WADI, BATADAL) are **attack-only** and not labelled for faults, so fault-vs-attack accuracy figures are rare and not comparable. A 99% score on a self-built simulator should be presented with that caveat.

### Cited Findings
- Urbina 2016 validates with simulations, the SWaT testbed and >200 GB of Modbus/TCP traces from a real US water facility (108 Modbus devices, 106 slave PLCs). Its metric is impact vs E[T_fa], not accuracy — [Urbina et al. 2016](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/%7Ealacarde/papers/ccs16.pdf)
- Urbina's survey of prior work: the vast majority use stateless tests, there is no consistent metric or adversary model, and none validated with all three of simulation, testbed and real data — [Urbina et al. 2016](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/%7Ealacarde/papers/ccs16.pdf)
- Xue 2025: simulation on a linearized UAV model and an RLC circuit, residual plots — [Xue et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14052)
- Ahsan 2026: five qualitative case studies on the GridAPPS-D DERMS code with synthesized events, explicitly **no accuracy/precision/recall/F1**, and a benchmark left to future work — [Ahsan et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03289)
- Zhang et al. IFAC 2022: simulation example only — [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/7263349)
- Power-system disturbance vs attack dataset (ORNL/MSU, Hink et al. 2014): 15 datasets from PMUs 1–4, relay Snort alarms and logs. A 2023 follow-up reports Random Forest at 90.56% accuracy versus logistic regression and k-NN — [arXiv 2307.03323](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.03323)
- BATADAL was built for attack detection only, with little work on separating attacks from other failures. Its metrics include *time to detect* — [AQUA review](https://iwa.silverchair.com/aqua/article-pdf/73/3/504/1392625/jws0730504.pdf); [ASCE JWRMD](https://ascelibrary.com/doi/10.1061/JWRMD5.WRENG-5302)
- SWaT labels samples "Attack" from attack launch rather than from when the process starts changing, which affects evaluation — [ICS testbed/dataset survey, arXiv 2102.05631](https://arxiv.org/html/2102.05631)
- Chockalingam et al. validated the BN's qualitative structure with domain experts rather than labelled incidents — [TU Delft](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:5926ebb8-9f98-4fd9-98cc-e670990def83)

### Inferences
- Suggested evaluation for our prototype, borrowing from the papers: (1) confusion matrix over the 10 event types plus "normal" (false alarm), not just binary accuracy; (2) **time-to-classify** (BATADAL-style); (3) **E[T_fa]** from long fault-free runs; (4) **Urbina impact metric** under an adaptive stealthy attacker (ramp-rate sweep: find the slowest FDI ramp that evades all rules and report the overflow margin); (5) randomized parameters (noise, leak size, onset time, Area) with multiple seeds so the 99% is not overfit to fixed scenarios; (6) a held-out "adversarial" set: drift-mimicking FDI, noisy replay, an attack spoofing both A and B, and a fault coinciding with an attack.

### Gaps
- No public benchmark with labelled *both* faults and attacks on a water-tank process was found. HAI and WADI were not examined for fault labels in this session.
- The original accuracy numbers of Hink et al. 2014 were not retrieved, only the 2023 follow-up's 90.56%.
